## HOW THE TRACES ARE LAID OUT

Each trace is a sequence of blocks.

  `===== ENVIRONMENT · not an agent turn =====`
      The task statement, tool or retrieval results, and other material
      produced by the environment, not by an agent.

  `===== TURN k · agent: NAME =====` ... `===== end of turn k =====`
      One agent turn, holding three sections in this order:
        `--- instructions given to this agent ---`   the agent's own instructions
        `--- input this agent received ---`          what it was handed
        `--- output this agent produced ---`         what it produced

  `===== OUTPUT WITHOUT PRECEDING INSTRUCTIONS ... =====`
      Assembled by the harness. Not a turn.

A turn is judged against ITS OWN instructions section and ITS OWN input
section, and nothing else. Instructions shown in another turn do not bind this
one, even when the agent has the same name in another trace. A mistake already
present in a turn's input belongs to the earlier turn that produced it, not to
this one.

The instructions section declares the agent's input and output fields and its
task. Producing a declared output field (a `reasoning` field listed under "Your
output fields") is required, not a deviation, even where a later sentence names
only some of the fields. The `[[ ## name ## ]]` markers that open each output
field are the harness's own output format, declared in that same section and
needed to parse the output; using them, rather than a plainer layout described
elsewhere in the instructions (`reasoning: ...`, `**query**: ...`), is required
and is never a failure.

### agent: create_query_hop2   (role: query_generator)
  receives: ['claim', 'summary_1']
  must produce: ['reasoning', 'query']
  requirements it was given:
    [c1] You are an AI assistant operating within a multi-hop fact-checking system.
    [c2] Your task is to generate a search query to retrieve evidence documents necessary to verify a given claim.
    [c3] You will be provided with the following inputs:
    [c4] `claim`: A factual claim that requires a three-hop retrieval trajectory to fully verify.
    [c5] `summary_1`: A summary of the information and evidence documents that have already been retrieved in the first hop.
    [c6] Given the fields `claim` and `summary_1`, produce the fields `reasoning` and `query`.
    [c7] 1. **Identify the Missing Evidence:** Compare `claim` with `summary_1` to determine what entities, facts, or relationships are still missing.
    [c8] Your generated query must bridge this gap to help the system retrieve the remaining required evidence documents in the next hop.
    [c9] 2. **Beware of Distractors or Incomplete Information:** Do not blindly trust that an entity mentioned in `summary_1` (e.g., "Paula Patton" or "Trent Reznor") is the only or correct subject of the claim.
    [c10] Often, the summary contains partial matches, and the actual required entity (e.g., "Toby Kebbell" or "Mariqueen Maandig") is still missing.
    [c11] Frame your query to explore the source or group (e.g., the full cast of a specific film, or the full list of producers) rather than anchoring strictly to the entity guessed in the summary.
    [c12] 3. **Avoid Yes/No Questions:** Do not formulate your query as a conversational or closed-ended question (e.g., "Did [Actor] act in [Film]?").
    [c13] Use concise, keyword-centric search phrases that are likely to match the text or title of an encyclopedia document.
    [c14] 4. **Avoid Complex Boolean Logic:** Do not use complex operators like `AND`, `OR`, or excessive quotation marks.
    [c15] Overly complex Boolean queries often fail to match the required documents.
    [c16] 5. **Use Specific Keywords from the Claim:** Ensure that the query incorporates the specific descriptive keywords from the claim used to identify the missing entity (e.g., "Peter Rees entertainment program creator", "The Veteran 2011 film actor", or "An Omen EP producer singer").
    [c17] Provide your response with the following fields:
    [c18] *reasoning**: Think step-by-step about what evidence is already available, what is missing, and how to best surface the remaining required documents without falling for distractor entities.
    [c19] *query**: A concise, keyword-based search query optimized to retrieve the missing evidence documents.

    [c18] If applicable, reiterate what missing information prevents full verification of the claim's comparisons or statements.

### agent: create_query_hop3   (role: query_generator)
  receives: ['claim', 'summary_1', 'summary_2']
  must produce: ['reasoning', 'query']
  requirements it was given:
    [c1] Given the fields `claim`, `summary_1`, `summary_2`, produce the fields `reasoning` and `query`.
    [c2] Your task is to generate a search query that will successfully retrieve *all* required evidence documents to verify a multi-hop claim.
    [c3] The retrieval trajectory typically involves multiple documents (e.g., three hops), and your query must find every single required document in this trajectory, including starting nodes, intermediate "bridge" nodes, and final nodes.
    [c4] Follow these guidelines to formulate your query:
    [c5] 1. **Include All Explicit Claim Entities and Concepts**: Deconstruct the claim into individual facts.
    [c6] Identify all explicit entities, descriptive phrases, and specialized concepts directly mentioned in the `claim` (e.g., "NorthCap University", "Nicanor Tiongson", "Owen Wilson", "Tom Wu", "Asymptotic safety in quantum gravity").
    [c7] Ensure all of these key terms are present in your query.
    [c8] 2. **Extract Bridge Entities from Summaries**: Identify intermediate or "bridge" entities mentioned in `summary_1` and `summary_2` that connect the facts in the claim (e.g., the film "Shanghai Knights" connecting two actors, or "University of the Philippines" connecting a person to their degree location).
    [c9] Add these bridge entities to your query.
    [c10] 3. **Address Indirect Descriptions**: If the claim describes an entity indirectly (e.g., "The Nobel Laureate in Physics known for his contributions with Abdus Salam"), ensure your query contains enough information to retrieve all potential matching entities (e.g., Sheldon Lee Glashow or Steven Weinberg), even if the summaries only mention one or miss them entirely.
    [c11] 4. **Always Generate a Query**: Even if the summaries are in complete agreement and fully verify the claim, you MUST generate a query.
    [c12] Your goal is to fetch the underlying evidence documents for the multi-hop trajectory, not just to answer or evaluate the claim.
    [c13] Never output "No query needed".
    [c14] 5. **Comprehensive Query**: Combine all identified explicit entities, bridge entities, and key conceptual phrases into a broad, comprehensive search query (or string of keywords) that will surface every required document.
    [c15] Provide your output strictly using the following format:
    [c16] reasoning: <Step-by-step reasoning identifying the explicit entities in the claim, the intermediate bridge entities from the summaries, and any missing links needed to cover all hops>
    [c17] query: <A comprehensive search query containing all necessary keywords and entity names>
