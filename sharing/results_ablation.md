# Sampling: how the results move with the tasks

Three checks on how much a number depends on which tasks were read. Formulas as in `formulas.md`, every kept instance
counted; tau-b with tied pairs dropped. Every draw is uniform without replacement with seeds 1 to 10, and every formula in a
table is scored on the same draws. Terminal-Bench is not included: its judged set has 19 usable tasks.

**A. Judged-side sweep** (which and how many judged tasks the formula reads)

1. Fix k. For seed 1 to 10, draw k of the judged tasks: `𝒯_k`. When k equals the judged set, use it once.
2. On `𝒯_k` only, compute every formula for every candidate, and gold on those k tasks (the bar of that draw).
3. Tau of each against the full generalization set's pass rates.
4. Report the mean tau over the draws, its range (min…max), and in how many draws the formula is at or above
   the same draw's gold.

**B. Generalization-side resampling** (how many generalization tasks the target is built from)

1. Score every candidate on the full judged set, once.
2. Fix m. For seed 1 to 10, draw m generalization tasks and recompute each candidate's pass rate on them.
3. Tau of each formula against that m-task target. Report the mean and range over draws; the gold column is
   gold on the judged tasks against the same m-task target.

**C. Disjoint judged sets** (a different judged set of the same size)

1. Take judged sets with no task in common: Models·HoVer's sets a and b (50 each); LiveCodeBench's judged split
   of 50 and the two halves of its 100-task split (the halves drawn with seed 0).
2. On each set, compute every formula and gold, and their tau against the full generalization set.

Generated by `scripts/sampling_tables.py`.
