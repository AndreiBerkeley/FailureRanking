## TASK

Take the traces ONE AT A TIME. For each, WALK THROUGH EVERY SUBSTANTIVE STEP THE
PROGRAM TOOK, IN ORDER, AND CHECK EACH ONE BEFORE MOVING TO THE NEXT.

A substantive step is any point where the program did work: a calculation, a
transformation, a comparison, an inference, an application of a rule or
definition, a judgement about the material, a decision about what to do next.

THE FIRST STEP IS ALWAYS INTAKE. Before anything else, check what this part of
the program understood from what it was handed. Did it read the task correctly?
Did it register every constraint and condition stated in it? Did it parse the
material it was given without corrupting or dropping any of it? A program that
starts from a misreading is wrong from that point on, however sound everything
after it looks.

ASSUME WHAT IT RECEIVED IS CORRECT, AND JUDGE ONLY WHAT IT DID WITH IT. If the
material handed to a part of the program was already wrong, acting on it is not
that part's mistake — it belongs to whatever produced it. But if the material was
correct and this part misread, mishandled or ignored it, that IS its mistake, and
it is exactly the kind this pass exists to catch.

RECOVERY DOES NOT EXCUSE A FAILURE. A mistake that a later step caught, worked
around, or happened to compensate for is still a mistake and is still reported.
Whether it survived to the end is a separate question from whether it occurred.

For each step, in order:
  1. State what the step did, in a few words.
  2. CHECK IT. Re-derive it yourself rather than reading it. Recompute the
     arithmetic. Confirm the fact is in the material this part was given. Test
     whether the conclusion follows from what precedes it. Confirm the rule,
     definition or principle invoked is stated correctly AND is the right one to
     invoke here. Verify the judgement against the material.
  3. Record "holds" or "fails". If it fails, say precisely what is wrong.

Do not skip a step because it looks routine — routine-looking steps are where
arithmetic and sign errors live. Do not stop at the first failure: later steps
can be independently wrong, and one wrong step does not excuse the rest from
being checked. A step that reads fluently can still be wrong, and fluent wrong
steps are the ones that matter.

You are NOT checking output formatting, required sections, or whether
instructions were followed. Ignore all of that here — it is examined separately,
and reporting it wastes this pass.

Report EVERY step that fails, including several in one trace, including small
ones, and including ones that did not change the outcome.

Return ONLY JSON. `steps_checked` is the number of substantive steps you walked,
and it must reflect the whole trace, not only the part where you found something:
